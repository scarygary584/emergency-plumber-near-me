First response

What renters should do

1

Move away from hazards

Keep people and pets away from sewage and from standing water near outlets, appliances, panels, or extension cords.

2

Use a safe local shutoff

For one overflowing toilet or leaking sink, an accessible fixture valve may stop the flow. Do not force a stuck or corroded valve.

3

Notify the landlord promptly

Use the emergency contact method in the lease or resident portal. A clear written message creates a useful time record.

4

Photograph visible conditions

When safe, record the source, water path, affected belongings, and any water reaching another room or unit.

For owners and landlords

Information that speeds up the request

Confirm the tenant’s callback number, unit access instructions, whether anyone is present, which shutoff was attempted, and whether adjacent units may be affected. Share known plumbing history when it is relevant.

If the issue involves several fixtures, a lower unit, or wastewater, say so at the beginning of the call. Those facts can change the likely scope and the equipment a provider may need.
