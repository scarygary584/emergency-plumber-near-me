Before help arrives

Reduce damage without making the problem worse

1

Shut off water when safe

Use the nearest fixture valve when possible. For a major leak, use the main shutoff only if you can reach it safely.

2

Avoid electrical hazards

Do not enter standing water near outlets, panels, appliances, or extension cords.

3

Stop using affected drains

During a sewer backup, stop flushing toilets and running sinks, washers, showers, and dishwashers.

4

Describe exactly what changed

Note when it started, which fixtures are affected, whether water is clean or dirty, and whether the shutoff worked.

Emergency plumbing FAQs

Is every leak a plumbing emergency?

No. A contained drip may be schedulable, while uncontrolled water, sewage, structural damage, or electrical danger usually deserves faster attention.

Should I turn off the main water valve?

For a major uncontrolled leak, shutting off the main can limit damage. Use it only when the valve is accessible and the area is safe.

What should I do during a sewer backup?

Stop using plumbing fixtures, keep people and pets away, avoid direct contact, and request professional help.

Do you guarantee immediate service?

No. Provider availability, response time, pricing, licensing, and service decisions vary. The call or form is used to collect request details and seek an available option.
