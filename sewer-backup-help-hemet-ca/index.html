Plumbing emergencies: first steps

  • Stop flushing and running water.
  • Pause laundry and dishwashing.
  • Keep clear of contaminated surfaces.
  • Stay away from water near electrical equipment.
  • Record where the overflow appeared first.

Do not enter standing water to reach a switch, outlet or electrical panel. If anyone is in immediate danger, contact 911.

Recognizing sewer backups

A single slow fixture may have a nearby obstruction. Water returning through several fixtures, especially at the lowest point in the building, can indicate a shared blockage farther along the system. Gurgling when another fixture empties is another useful detail to report. These observations help explain the situation, but they do not establish the location or cause without an inspection.

A sewage odor alone does not prove that a pipe has failed. Describe whether you can see liquid, whether it appears only during water use, and whether the problem has happened before. Do not keep flushing or running taps to reproduce the symptoms. Each test can add more water where it cannot escape.

Limit contact and stop adding water

Treat a sewage backup as contaminated, even when the liquid looks fairly clear. Avoid touching it, and keep children and animals away. Move to a dry area rather than walking through the spill to retrieve belongings. The CDC advises avoiding contact with water contaminated by sewage.

Stop using sinks, showers, toilets and appliances that discharge water. Tell other occupants so they do not accidentally restart a cycle. Do not add drain chemicals or mix cleaning products. If a chemical has already been used, tell the responding technician what it was and when it was added. Leave cleanout caps closed; opening one may release backed-up liquid.

Inspection and clearing options

Before authorizing work, ask how the provider plans to identify the obstruction and access the affected piping. The first visit may involve checking fixture behavior, locating an accessible cleanout and deciding whether a camera inspection would be useful. Tell the technician about earlier blockages, recent renovations and any previous pipe work you know about.

Drain cleaning may address a local restriction, while sewer cleaning may be discussed for a blockage farther downstream. The appropriate equipment depends on access, pipe condition and what is causing the obstruction. Ask why a method is suitable for your property rather than assuming every clog needs the same treatment.

If trouble keeps returning, ask whether inspection found roots, buildup, a damaged joint or another defect. Clearing an obstruction and completing structural repairs are different scopes of work. A camera may help document the condition once the line is accessible, but availability and the usefulness of an inspection must be confirmed by the provider.

Overflow cleanup and property recovery

Restoring flow does not automatically make an affected room ready to use. Ask whether the responding company handles contaminated water removal and surface treatment or whether a separate restoration provider is needed. Do not assume an emergency plumber also performs damage cleanup, drying, flooring removal or replacement of affected materials.

Explain which surfaces were reached: tile, carpeting, walls, cabinets or stored belongings. A professional can assess what can be cleaned and what needs further treatment or removal. Avoid using household fans to spread air from a contaminated area into other rooms. Keep access restricted until the affected space has been assessed and appropriate treatment is complete.

From a dry, safe location, note when you first noticed the problem and take photographs if possible. Keep the provider's findings, invoices and any restoration records together. If you plan to contact an insurer, ask what documentation is needed and what your policy covers; coverage should not be assumed from the presence of water alone.

Requesting help in Hemet, CA

Have your street address, callback number and property type ready. Include the unit number for an apartment or multi-unit building, and mention locked gates, pets or access restrictions. A Hemet mailing address alone does not confirm the responding provider's coverage. Give the exact location within Riverside County and ask for availability before relying on an arrival time.

  • Say whether water is still rising or has stopped.
  • List the affected fixtures and rooms.
  • Explain whether neighboring units have similar symptoms.
  • Mention any known cleanout location without opening it.
  • Identify who can authorize access and work.

For a rental, notify the landlord or property manager promptly and follow the property's reporting process. For a business, explain whether restrooms or other essential facilities are unusable. Confirm the provider's identity, licensing where applicable, proposed scope and authorization process before work begins.

When a public-system report may be needed

If you see an overflow at a street manhole or suspect a main sewer problem beyond the property, report the location to the utility serving that address. Keep clear and do not lift public covers. The City of Hemet water and wastewater page lists its emergency number as (951) 765-2400. This is a city contact, separate from this website's request line. Confirm the responsible utility if your address uses a different service district.
